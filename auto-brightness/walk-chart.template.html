Auto-Brightness Walk

Twenty minutes carrying the laptop between a dark room, a badly lit room, a well lit room and outdoors, setting the brightness by hand at each stop. Automatic brightness was off. The recorded light readings are then replayed through GNOME's algorithm and two replacements. Each one gets the 6% chosen in the badly lit room at the start, and is left alone after that.

Typical miss is the median factor between the algorithm's backlight and the one chosen by hand, over the nine "looks good" moments after the start. Lag is how much later than the light sensor the backlight gets 90% of the way to its new level, one number per move. Timer wakeups are extra wakeups beyond the sensor's own updates; in parentheses, how many fell while standing still.

How to read this

Stock GNOME 50 is a port of iio_proxy_changed() from gnome-settings-daemon 50.0, combined the way GNOME Shell 50.1 does it: backlight = gsd's target + slider − 50%. Right after you set the slider, gsd maps the current light to a 66.7% target, so with the slider at its minimum the backlight can't go below about 17%. That's why stock starts at 16.7% instead of 6%. It ignores readings of 0, but readings just above 0 in the dark push it to 0%.

Fixed timing keeps GNOME's mapping and Shell's slider bias, and only replaces the smoothing: each reading counts for the time it actually held, a 10 s time constant, a 2-point dead zone, and no polling.

Power law also replaces the mapping with backlight = 6% × (reading / start reading)0.4, with a 1% floor that also applies when the sensor reads 0. It smooths on a log scale with a 10% dead zone, brightening with a 3 s time constant and dimming with 10 s. The exponent was fitted to this same walk, so its accuracy here is flattering. Its steadiness, lag and wakeup counts don't depend on that fit.

The sensor values are not really lux: outdoors on a mild afternoon they reach 1.5 million. In the dark room the sensor reads 0, drawn at the bottom edge of the top panel.
